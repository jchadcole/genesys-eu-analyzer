How it works

Genesys Cloud charges AI features in Experience Units (EUs). The analyzer works in two steps, one tab each.

1 · EU consumptionDescribe your org, your AI seats and how customers move through self-service. You get the EUs used per month and what you'll pay for them.
2 · Break-evenEvery self-service path that uses AI becomes a use case. For each one, you see how much of it the AI must resolve before it pays for itself.
Executive reportA two-page summary of the business case, ready to print or save as PDF.

The page opens with a worked example of a 200-agent contact center. Change any number and every result updates right away. Nothing is sent anywhere: your inputs stay in your own browser.

This is a planning model, not a Genesys bill. Rates come from Genesys's published Experience Unit model and are dated in the analyzer's header.

What to gather first

You can start with estimates and refine them later. A month of reporting data is ideal.

  • Your Genesys Cloud license (CX 1–4, named or concurrent) and the number of agents
  • Monthly voice calls and digital interactions (web chat, messaging) that go through self-service
  • How those interactions split across routes, for example IVR only, IVR then a bot, or a bot then a virtual agent
  • Average minutes a call spends in self-service, and in the bot in particular
  • Average agent handle time for each type of interaction, in minutes
  • Fully loaded monthly cost of one agent, paid hours, shrinkage and occupancy
  • Any negotiated EU price or discount, platform fees and implementation cost
  • Seats for per-user AI features such as Agent Copilot, if you plan to buy them

Self-service use cases

An interaction can pass through up to four self-service tiers. Genesys bills it only at the highest tier it reaches, so one call that goes IVR → bot → VA costs the VA price and nothing else.

Fixed elements and other usage elements

Fixed elements are per-seat features such as Agent Copilot and Speech and Text Analytics. Enter the number of users who will have each one. Other usage elements are volume-based features such as AI Scoring, Predictive Routing and messaging channels. Enter your monthly volumes.

These elements count in your Billable EU total. They are not part of the self-service break-even, because they don't replace agent time on an interaction.

Reading Step 2 results

The use-case table

Each row compares two costs for one interaction:

  • AI cost: the EUs it uses times your EU price, plus its share of any IVR overage. It's charged whether or not the AI resolves the interaction.
  • Agent cost: what an agent's time on that interaction costs.

Break-even containment = AI cost ÷ agent cost. If an AVA interaction costs 1.18 and an agent would cost 12.04, the AVA has to resolve 9.8% of those interactions to pay for itself.

The bar shows how the target compares with break-even:

Green bar past the line: the target beats break-even, and the gap is your headroom.
Orange bar short of the line: the AI needs more containment, a lower tier, or a longer handle time to justify it.

If a row says Can't break even, the AI costs more per interaction than the agent does, so no amount of containment pays it back.

Net / month is shown in agent hours first, with the money amount below. It's the labor saved on contained interactions minus the AI cost on all of them.

The executive report

Click Executive report at the top of the analyzer. It always reflects your current inputs.

  • Page 1, the value: total savings over the years you chose, a one-paragraph summary, savings per year, agent FTEs freed, payback, and a break-even vs target bar for each use case (up to nine).
  • Page 2, supporting details: program break-even, a table of every use case, savings year by year, how the monthly savings add up, key assumptions and the method.

Click Print / save as PDF, then choose "Save as PDF" as the printer. You get two US Letter pages. Press Esc or Close to return.

Questions

Why do AVA use cases need higher containment?

AVA is billed 1.2 EU on every interaction that reaches it, whether or not it resolves it. A bot is billed by the minute or session and usually costs far less per interaction.

Why isn't Agent Copilot in the break-even?

Per-seat features are a flat charge that doesn't depend on how many interactions the AI resolves. They appear in the Billable totals on Step 1 and are left out of break-even, so they never change it.

A use case can't break even. What can I change?

Move it to a lower tier (for example VA instead of AVA), keep AVA in its own flow so fewer interactions enter it, or check that the agent handle time includes after-call work.

Why does the tool say my shares don't total 100%?

The paths for a channel should account for all of its interactions. The tool scales them to 100% so the results still work, but correct them for accuracy.

Glossary

Experience Unit (EU)
The unit Genesys Cloud uses to bill AI features. Formerly called AI Experience tokens.
IVR
Interactive voice response: menus and prompts. No EU charge.
Bot
A scripted voice or digital bot flow.
VA
Virtual Agent: an AI agent that answers in natural language.
AVA
Agentic Virtual Agent: an AI agent that can take actions to resolve a request.
Containment
The share of interactions resolved in self-service without reaching an agent.
Handle time
Agent minutes per interaction, including after-call work.
FTE
Full-time equivalent: one full-time agent's worth of working time.
Fair-use minutes
Your org's pooled IVR minutes per month, based on license and users.
Free allocation
EUs included with your license each month. They don't carry over.
